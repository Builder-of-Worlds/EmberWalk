When the next wildfire comes, will your home be ready?

How homes catch fire

It usually starts with an ember

Most homes lost to wildfire are never touched by the wall of flames. Research by IBHS, the insurance industry's building safety lab, found that embers cause up to 90 percent of home ignitions.

Source: IBHS news release, Jul. 13th, 2020.

Embers ride the wind and land in the dry leaves, the wood pile by the wall, the fence that touches the house. The small things around your home can decide what happens to it.

In the 2025 Los Angeles fires, homes with all four of a fire-rated roof, fire-resistant siding, double-pane windows and enclosed eaves came through undamaged 54 percent of the time. Homes with only one: 36 percent.

What California requires

The first five feet matter most

California's defensible space law asks for up to 100 feet of cleared space around homes in the state's fire hazard areas, split into three zones. The closest, 0 to 5 feet from the walls, is known as Zone 0. The Board of Forestry adopted rules for it in August 2026; they are not in effect yet. The IBHS findings above show why they matter.

Public Resources Code 4291; Board of Forestry Zone 0 rulemaking, Aug. 2026.

0 to 5 feet

Nothing that can burn against the house: no bark mulch, dry plants, firewood or wood fence touching the walls. Gravel, stone and bare soil are the safe choices here.

5 to 30 feet

Keep it lean and green: clear dead leaves and branches, space out shrubs, trim trees away from the roof and keep grass short.

30 to 100 feet

Thin it out: mow dry grass, space trees and shrubs apart, and remove low branches so fire can't climb or run toward the house.
